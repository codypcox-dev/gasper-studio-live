# NORTHSTAR — GASPER-V4

**Id:** `GASPER-V4-001`  
**Engine:** TriForce 4.0.0  
**Priority:** P0 identity recovery, then studio expansion  

## The result

The next meaningful run must show **real Gasper in 3D**, not a plausible blob.

At the Wispwalker gate, the user should immediately recognize the canonical character from the old vector rig from the front, then see that exact organism hold together through quarter, profile, back-three-quarter, and full orbit. The surface must feel like one dense, viscoelastic dark-pearl body with continuous feet/nubs grown from the same mass. It may deform and breathe, but it may not become a rounded candy, mint, capsule, pill, generic ghost, or inflated logo.

## North-star proof sequence

The first v4 proof is one uninterrupted capture:

1. Canonical Wispwalker front lock.
2. Slow 360° orbit with no silhouette pop or component split.
3. Face remains attached to a true surface patch and disappears by real occlusion, not by a view switch.
4. Blink, gaze/saccade, mouth expression, and one asymmetric social read.
5. Breathing/volume idle without identity swelling.
6. Two planted weight transfers with visible loaded/free lower lobes.
7. A turn through true 3/4 volume.
8. `neutral-settled → listening-receive → thinking-knit → blocked-strain → recovering → pleased-soft → neutral-settled` with interruption from current interpolated values.
9. Material/key light travels over the actual rotating surface.
10. Grid/rig overlay proves authoring controls are attached to the same organism.

## Definition of real 3D

Real 3D means:

- one watertight closed surface;
- actual XYZ vertices/normals and perspective projection;
- an authored front/back depth field with a deliberate profile silhouette;
- no alternate 2D silhouette swapped in by yaw;
- no extrusion/card thickness trick;
- no radial one-pole assumption;
- no convex-hull substitute for concave anatomy;
- true self-occlusion and moving specular response;
- deterministic correspondence from canonical semantic controls to render vertices.

## Identity before generality

v4 does not begin by building a universal character system. It begins by perfectly authoring Gasper.

Order:

1. Wispwalker volume.
2. Wispwalker vector face on surface.
3. Wispwalker living motion.
4. Wispwalker 360° visual acceptance.
5. Presence and remaining embodiments as projections of the same identity.
6. 20-second scene recreated in 3D.
7. Studio tooling expanded around the proven rig.

## Visual laws

- Front silhouette is not a suggestion. It is a lock at the canonical view.
- Profile is authored, not inferred from a generic inflation formula.
- The lower W/cleft reads as two load-bearing lobes from the front, not as two separate tubes in 3D.
- The crown is one continuous mass; no cloud-on-bowl split.
- Body depth is regionally varied: crown, face/body, lower mass, and plants are not one uniform thickness.
- The face is embedded into the organism, never pasted onto a flat card.
- Internal energy supports expression and form; it does not replace them.
- Motion changes weight, tension, momentum, and timing before it changes spectacle.

## Studio north star

Gasper Studio should feel like a highly simplified character-specific Blender/Rive hybrid:

- direct semantic handles first;
- raw vertices available only when intentionally entering advanced edit mode;
- one hierarchy, one selection model, one timeline, one graph;
- contextual panels rather than duplicated workspaces;
- every visible control is live, serializable, undoable, keyframeable, and inspectable;
- author the character while seeing the final result, not a detached proxy.

## Stop conditions

Stop the lane and repair if any of these appear:

- pressed mint / candy / capsule read;
- front silhouette drift;
- flat-card profile;
- two-component body;
- feet become separate tubes;
- face floats, slides, rasterizes, or ignores surface perspective;
- 25×40 grid becomes the body topology again;
- a worker invents a second Gasper mesh;
- orbit and identity yaw have separate shape writers;
- a PASS is claimed without full-orbit capture and owner review.
